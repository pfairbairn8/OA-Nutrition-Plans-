01 Add Snacks Between Meals This is the main lever

Your three main meals are in reasonable shape and protein intake across the day is likely already sufficient. What is missing is food in between. There are two windows to work with: mid-morning between breakfast and lunch, and mid-afternoon between lunch and dinner. Carbohydrate-focused options are the priority — bagels with honey, cereal bars, flapjacks, fruit and nut bars, or a smoothie all work well. The snacking section gives you a straightforward way to pick the right option depending on what you need in that moment.

One thing to keep in mind

You mentioned avoiding eating close to bed because of how it affects sleep, and that is sensible to maintain. The aim is to front-load the extra intake earlier in the day rather than rely on eating late to hit the numbers.

02 Use Smoothies as an Easy Calorie Top-Up

A smoothie is one of the most efficient ways to add calories and a ton of micronutrients without it feeling like another meal. A simple approach: a base (oats, yogurt, or banana), a liquid (milk or juice), and extras depending on timing and what you need (fruit, honey, nut butter, avocado). Two minutes to make, can be prepared the night before if mornings are tight, and easily portable. Mid-morning works well, or alongside breakfast if the window is there.

03 Stack Snacks With Existing Routine Cues

With a schedule that shifts as much as yours does, fixed eating times won't always be reliable. Try to attach eating to things that already happen fairly consistently — specific points in your day that remain relatively stable regardless of what else is going on. You know your routine better than anyone, so you know which points those are. The aim is to make one or two feeding opportunities automatic: e.g. something in your bag before you leave or a bar strapped to a water bottle.

The Principle
MAKE IT
AUTOMATIC

Attach eating to events that already happen. Remove the decision from the moment. One or two consistent feeding opportunities beats five inconsistent ones.
