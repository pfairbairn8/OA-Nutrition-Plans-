01 Build Two Main Meals Within eating window

The most important change. Current intake is well under 1,000kcal on most days — that level of restriction makes hunger and overeating later almost inevitable and does not support training.

Two main meals in the eating window, plus one or two snacks. Each meal needs a proper protein source, a carbohydrate, and vegetables. Frame it as "how much nutrition can I get" — not how low can I go.

Protein Target · Per Main Meal
30–40 g protein

Chicken: 150–200g cooked. Scrambled eggs on toast: 3 eggs (~20g) + tin of beans = ~30g with good fibre. No need to count — just include a proper source.

02 Switch Your Soups 350–400kcal target
What to look for

350–400kcal. High protein and fibre. Chicken and vegetable or lentil-based. Baxters, New Covent Garden, Tesco Finest, M&S all have strong options.

How to use them

Works in a flask on driving days. Pair with a bread roll — now it is a proper meal, not a placeholder. The very-low-calorie soups are not doing the job.

03 Protein Shake Snack — not a meal
Keep it at 17:30

The protein shake works well as a snack between meals. Keep it where it is. Do not use it as a meal replacement.

Pair it

Add a piece of fruit or a couple of rice cakes. Carbohydrate alongside the protein makes it more satisfying.

Pop Chips & Diet Coke

These tools can help manage cravings — that is not a problem. They work better as add-ons when the main meals are already solid, rather than as the primary strategy.

04 Structured Diet Breaks Every 3 weeks

Regular breaks from a deficit rather than running one continuously. Three weeks of consistent dieting, then a minimum of three days at maintenance. This gives a psychological reset and helps re-establish appetite before the next block.

Phase Duration Approach
Deficit block ~3 weeks Consistent eating as outlined. Track start date.
Maintenance break 3+ days Same foods — slightly larger portions or add breakfast back. Not a free-for-all.
Building the skill

Given a history of weight cycling through restriction and gain, practising eating at maintenance with solid habits already in place is genuinely useful. Think of it as building a skill — not a reward. You decide when to take the break. Keep a note of when you start each block.

Target Cycle
3 weeks on → 3 days min off

05 Dietary Flexibility

People sit somewhere along a spectrum in how they approach food — from more rigid to more flexible. This is not a fixed category and can shift over time depending on situation, environment, and emotional state.

Rigid Balanced Flexible
Rigid Dietary Restraint Flexible Dietary Restraint
All-or-nothing approach Moderate, consistent approach
Eliminates 'forbidden' foods, narrowing variety Focus on choices — not rules
One 'cheat' triggers further overeating — the day is already written off No foods entirely off limits — think frequency, portion, context
Linked to increased impulsiveness and setbacks More likely to reach long-term goals
→ Shifting the Language
Move away from

"Cheating." "Cheat days." The cheat-and-diet cycle is exactly the pattern that keeps people stuck. Catch yourself when you use this framing.

Think instead

Frequency. Portion. Context. The odd fish and chips from the chippy does not derail anything when the week as a whole is consistent.

Key Principle
NO FOODS

No foods should be entirely off limits. Instead: how often, how much, and in what context. After one unplanned choice — continue with the next planned meal as normal. Do not compensate by restricting. Keep overall consistency intact.

06 Simple Meals Plug-and-play framework

Healthy eating does not need complex recipes or long prep times. Put together four components. That is the whole system.

Component Options
Protein Chicken, fish, eggs, mince, Quorn, prawns, tinned tuna/salmon, lean beef mince (5%)
Carbohydrate Rice, pasta, potatoes, wraps, bread, noodles
Vegetables Fresh, frozen, or pre-prepped. Frozen counts fully. A portion = a handful or small tin.
Flavour Sauce, seasoning, spices, dressing — jarred is fine.
Chinese / Stir-Fry
  • Stir-fry veg bag
  • Rice or noodles
  • Chicken (batch-cooked ahead)
  • Packet stir-fry sauce
Italian / Pasta
  • Pasta or Pasta n Sauce as a base
  • 150–200g cooked chicken or meatballs
  • Jarred tomato sauce
  • Handful of frozen spinach stirred through
Jacket Potato
  • Microwaveable jacket (10–12 min)
  • Tin of beans, leftover chicken, or tuna
  • Salad or pre-prepped veg on the side
Scrambled Eggs
  • 2–3 eggs scrambled
  • 2 slices of toast
  • Tin of beans on the side (~30g protein total)
Cook once, eat twice

Cook a batch of chicken or mince in the evening and portion half for the next day before you eat. Same amount of cooking. Two meals out of it. 5 portions of fruit and veg per day — not 5 of fruit. Frozen counts. A portion is a handful or small tin.

07 Eating on the Go Driving days · Plan A / B / C

On driving days, decisions become reactive when you are already hungry. Pick a plan before you set off — not when food is in front of you. A subsidy is available: the budget is there to eat well.

A
Supermarket or Coolbox
  • Pre-made soup (350–400kcal, chicken or lentil) — works in a flask from home
  • High-protein salad pot or meal deal with protein filling
  • Cooked chicken slices or Skyr / Greek yogurt
  • Banana or apple
  • Popcorn or baked crisps as savoury snack
B
M&S Stop
  • High-protein salad pot or large salad
  • Cooked or marinated chicken to add protein
  • Piece of fruit
  • Popcorn or baked crisps
C
Fast Food
  • Grilled chicken wrap (sweet chilli, tikka, or BBQ)
  • Medium fries or carrot sticks
  • Apple slices
Quick Check · Before You Set Off
3 questions
  • 01 Which plan am I using today — A, B, or C?
  • 02 What will I have for the main, snack, and drink?
  • 03 Decide this before food is in front of you.
