01 Overview

The main focus of our conversation was on building a handful of consistent habits that fit into your current life rather than requiring you to structure your life around them. Energy was the primary goal we agreed on, with gradual weight loss as a secondary outcome that tends to follow when the basics are in place.

The approach below is deliberately straightforward. There is a lot that could be addressed, but trying to change too many things at once is one of the most reliable ways to change nothing. The aim is to identify a small number of things that hold even when you're busy, tired, or away, and build from there.

02 Your Goals
  • → Improve day-to-day energy
  • → Build 2–3 consistent eating habits that hold regardless of what is going on at home
  • → Bring more structure to meals and reduce grazing through the day
  • → Lose some weight gradually as a by-product of getting the above in place
The Focus
HABITS FIRST.
OUTCOMES FOLLOW.

Energy and gradual weight loss are the targets. Consistent, simple habits are the mechanism. Structure your day around the habits — not the other way round.

03 Key Actions
01 Work towards three consistent meals per day

The current pattern of grabbing food when there is a window, eating late after the kids are down, and grazing in between is currently not fully serving you. Three consistent meals, even imperfect ones, will reduce the amount of food decisions you are making throughout the day and quieten the background noise that tends to lead to snacking.

Start with breakfast. On Sunday evening, make a large batch of overnight oats — a single container covers the full week, or you can do individual portions with slightly different flavours. Oats, milk, or a milk alternative, Greek yogurt, and a handful of fresh or frozen fruit is all it takes. You can also add nuts and seeds for more micronutrients and fibre, there are lots of ways you can customise and change the flavour from day to day or week to week. They are ready to go in the morning with no decisions involved. From there, work towards a consistent lunch and an earlier evening meal. The Healthy Eating Anchors section gives a framework for building this in with your partner.

02 Cook once, eat twice

Your wife's cooking is already one of the strongest things going. The aim is not to change what she is making, it is to deliberately scale it so there is always something ready the following day. For the big pot meals you already make: make them bigger, portion them out, and lunch is covered. Curries, stews, chilli, pasta dishes — all of these scale well and hold well in the fridge for two to three days. The Simple Meal Frameworks section gives some quick plug-and-play options for when variety is needed without the prep overhead.

03 Set process goals and co-create them with your partner

We talked about the putting your running shoes on example — the goal is putting the trainers on, not running 5km every day. Scaling goals to the smallest meaningful action should not be viewed as setting the bar low; it is removing the main reason that momentum stalls. When a target is achievable on a bad day, there is no such thing as a failed week or day. Setting targets at a level where missing them becomes a source of frustration tends to be what drives the burn-out cycle.

The other part of this is presenting goals to your partner as suggestions rather than a plan you have already decided on. Things that are co-created tend to hold up better than things that are self-imposed, particularly in a shared household. "What do you think about trying..." lands differently to "I am going to..." and is more likely to get you pulling in the same direction.

04 Healthy Eating Anchors Continued
What Are Anchors?

Anchors are 2–3 non-negotiable habits that:

  • —Fit into your current routine
  • —Support your goals
  • —Can be repeated consistently, even during busy or difficult periods
Why This Matters

Motivation and ideal conditions are not reliable. A small number of anchors keeps things moving even when everything else is less structured. Having something fixed in place reduces decision-making, maintains momentum, and prevents the complete drop-off that tends to happen during busy or stressful periods.

How Your Anchors Were Chosen

The anchors below were selected based on three questions:

  • —What is already happening most days that can be built on rather than replaced?
  • —Where are the biggest gaps between what is currently happening and what would move things forward?
  • —What will still be achievable on a difficult day?

These were discussed in the consultation and are intended as starting points to agree with your partner rather than a unilateral decision. Importantly, you don't have to have the same 3 anchors — you could agree on 1–2 to both commit to, so you have some shared goals and can support each other, and 1 each that you do that is more for you based on your own specific needs.

Anchor 1 — Two glasses of water first thing in the morning

Hydration was already on your radar so let's address it first thing with a straightforward solution.

Anchor 2 — Breakfast within an hour of waking

Overnight oats made on Sunday evening means this costs zero effort in the morning. This is the one anchor that changes the pattern of the day the most — eating early reduces the grazing that builds through the morning.

Anchor 3 — Your choice

Set your own final anchor based on what you want to achieve and based on that mindset of minimum effective dose to improve energy and overall nutrition.

How to Use These

Focus on getting these in place consistently before working on anything else. Other areas can be improved over time, but these should remain stable even when things are busy. They are worth coming back to during difficult periods as a check — if these are happening, things are on track.

05 Simple Meal Frameworks

Part of our discussion touched on how easy it is to end up eating whatever is available when the day has already been long and the kids have finally gone to bed. Healthy eating does not need to involve complex recipes, long prep times, or starting from scratch every day. In most cases, it comes down to putting together a few simple components.

Simple Meal Framework

Most meals can be built using a very simple structure:

  • →Protein — chicken, mince, fish, eggs, beans
  • →Carbohydrate — rice, pasta, potatoes, wraps, bread
  • →Vegetables — fresh, frozen, pre-prepped
  • →Flavour — sauce, seasoning, spices

Meal Frameworks — use these as plug-and-play options. All scale well and make portions for the following day.

Stir-Fry
  • —Stir-fry veg bag
  • —Rice or noodles
  • —Chicken, prawns, or beef
  • —Packet stir-fry sauce
Mexican
  • —Peppers and onions (fresh, frozen, or pre-chopped)
  • —Chicken or beef mince
  • —Wraps or rice
  • —Fajita spice mix
Italian
  • —Pasta or filled pasta (ravioli/tortellini)
  • —Beef mince or chicken
  • —Jarred tomato sauce
  • —Frozen spinach or med veg stirred in
Curry
  • —Chicken, chickpeas, or lentils
  • —Jarred curry sauce
  • —Microwave rice or naan
  • —Frozen spinach, peas, or mixed veg
Key Principles
  • →Convenience foods are a useful tool — not something to always avoid
  • →A few reliable go-to options will make a bigger difference than trying new recipes every week
  • →Almost all of these will make 2+ portions — portion out the rest for lunch the next day
